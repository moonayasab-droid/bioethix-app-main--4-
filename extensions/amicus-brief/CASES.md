# Structured Case Analysis & Ethical Dilemma Framework

## What is a Case?

A case is a real or realistic healthcare scenario involving ethical conflict, legal uncertainty, or policy questions. Cases are used to teach bioethics principles and legal reasoning.

## Case Structure

Each case includes:

### 1. Ethical Dilemmas
Identify conflicts between core principles:
- Autonomy: The patient's right to make their own medical decisions
- Beneficence: The duty to maximize patient benefit
- Non-maleficence: The obligation to avoid harm
- Justice: Fair and equitable treatment

### 2. Competing Perspectives
Examine viewpoints from:
- Patient perspective
- Provider perspective
- Family/guardian perspective
- State/legal perspective
- Resolution pathways

### 3. Multi-Principle Analysis
Users can analyze the same case through different ethical lenses:

#### Autonomy Pathway
- Does the patient have decision-making capacity?
- Was informed consent obtained?
- Are there constraints on the patient's choice?

#### Beneficence Pathway
- What treatment maximizes overall benefit to the patient?
- How do we weigh quality of life versus length of life?

#### Non-maleficence Pathway
- What harms are involved in each option?
- How can we minimize harm while pursuing benefit?

#### Justice Pathway
- Are resources distributed fairly?
- Does the patient receive equal access to care?

## Case Analysis Workflow

1. Present the facts
2. Identify the dilemma
3. Analyze each principle
4. Compare perspectives
5. Consider jurisdiction
6. Discuss resolution
7. Note uncertainties

## Example Case Template

**Case Title**: Pediatric Treatment Refusal

**Facts**:
- A 16-year-old patient refuses the recommended treatment
- Family and patient hold a religious objection to certain care
- The medical team believes the treatment is necessary
- Legal and ethical questions arise

**Ethical Dilemmas**:
1. Autonomy: Does the patient have the right to refuse?
2. Beneficence: What treatment best promotes benefit?
3. Non-maleficence: What harms arise from treatment or refusal?
4. Justice: Are there fair and equitable ways to resolve conflict?

**Competing Perspectives**:
- Patient: values autonomy and conscience
- Provider: focuses on medical necessity and risk
- Family: prioritizes values and care responsibilities
- State: may intervene to protect life or public interest

**Resolution Pathways**:
- Ethics consultation
- Enhanced communication
- Alternative treatment evaluation
- Judicial review if required
