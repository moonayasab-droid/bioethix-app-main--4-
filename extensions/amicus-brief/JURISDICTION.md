# Jurisdiction Comparison: US, EU, UAE

## Overview

This document outlines how bioethics principles and patient rights are addressed in three major jurisdictions: the United States (US), European Union (EU), and United Arab Emirates (UAE). Each jurisdiction has different legal frameworks, ethical standards, and policy approaches.

Important: Legal frameworks are updated regularly. The information here is dated to 2024 and should be verified with current sources for specific decisions.

---

## United States

### Legal Foundations

- 14th Amendment: Due process and equal protection
- HIPAA: Patient privacy and confidentiality
- ADA: Disability rights and healthcare access
- State law variation: informed consent, end-of-life considerations, and healthcare decision-making

### Key Legal Themes

- Informed consent
- Capacity assessment and surrogate decision-making
- Patient autonomy and refusal of treatment
- Reproductive rights and privacy
- Access, discrimination, and equity

### Representative Sources

- U.S. Department of Health & Human Services
- U.S. Supreme Court opinions
- State supreme court and appellate decisions
- Federal and state statutes

---

## European Union

### Legal Foundations

- Charter of Fundamental Rights
- GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)
- Oviedo Convention on Human Rights and Biomedicine
- Member state law and professional regulation

### Key Legal Themes

- Data protection and health privacy
- Patient autonomy and dignity
- Informed consent and capacity
- Reproductive rights and healthcare access
- Cross-border care and rights protections

### Representative Sources

- European Commission
- Council of Europe
- European Court of Human Rights
- EU directives and member-state implementation rules

---

## United Arab Emirates

### Legal Foundations

- UAE Constitution
- Federal healthcare laws and regulations
- Emirate-level healthcare and professional codes
- Islamic jurisprudence and bioethical principles

### Key Legal Themes

- Patient consent and family-centered decision-making
- Public health responsibilities and healthcare access
- Professional obligations and medical ethics
- Reproductive and end-of-life legal questions
- Integration of Sharia and civil regulatory principles

### Representative Sources

- UAE Ministry of Health and Prevention
- Dubai Health Authority
- SEHA / Abu Dhabi health systems
- Federal legal databases and health regulations

## Comparative Summary

| Issue | US | EU | UAE |
|-------|----|----|-----|
| Informed consent | Required | Required | Required |
| Right to refuse treatment | Recognized | Recognized | Recognized with conditions |
| Data protection | HIPAA + state law | GDPR | Emerging frameworks |
| End-of-life care | State-based | Country-based | Guided by Islamic and medical standards |
| Healthcare access | Varies by system | Generally broader access | Government + employer/private model |
